01 / Objective

Separating mechanisms that are often confused.

A reachable path does not guarantee a time-valid path. A time-valid path does not guarantee transmission. And a graph model is not necessarily better at forecasting than a simple history baseline. This research tested these questions separately rather than interpreting every difference as “network influence.”

STRUCTURE

Weak connection is not mutual reachability

The main weakly connected component contained 17,598 users and 29,715 directed edges. Its largest strongly connected component contained just 17 users, highlighting how much changes when arrow direction is respected.

TEMPORAL EVIDENCE

Rebuild interactions without edge timestamps

Extracted retweets, inferred replies and mentions from tweet text to reconstruct 129,604 timestamped interaction events. Modelled the stored direction as author → referenced account, not as proven causal information flow.

CLASS LABELS

Avoid over-interpreting Target 0 and 1

The two dataset classes were treated as operational labels, not verified political stance, misinformation, ideology or truth. Lexical sentiment comparisons were secondary checks, not ground-truth validation.

TEMPORAL GRAPHS

Time-respecting paths and forecasting

Constructed 25 Monday-based weekly network snapshots and earliest-arrival reachability analyses. Forecast features were restricted to information preceding each target week to avoid leakage.

02 / Experimental design

Built a multi-stage testing pipeline.

The study combines descriptive network analysis with stochastic controls, ablations, temporal simulation and forecasting benchmarks.

A1 / CHRONOLOGY

Randomized-time null models

Compared observed time-respecting retention with timestamp-shuffled and alternative randomized networks. After Holm multiple-testing correction, observed chronology was not statistically established as unusually restrictive.

A3 / PROPAGATION

Calibrated R-eff and null graphs

Measured eligible-edge probabilities, empirically calibrated first-generation reproduction, and compared the observed graph against 100 validated directed degree-preserving rewired graphs at matched transmission-strength targets.

A7 / KERNEL ATTRIBUTION

Shapley-style modifier analysis

The supplied research summary reports complete coalition analyses of the transmission kernel. It distinguished the influence of edge-weight normalization from additional centrality and orientation modifiers.

A2 / EVENT-DRIVEN SIMULATION

Transmit only when an event occurs

Built an event-driven variant that requires an interaction strictly after activation. Repeated interactions use event-level probabilities that preserve cumulative pair probability when all events are available.

04 / Event-driven diffusion

Static paths and actual interaction opportunities are different.

REPEATED-EVENT CORRECTION

Preserving pair-level probability

For a pair with n observed events, the research used p_event = 1 − (1 − p_pair)^(1/n) rather than applying the full pair probability independently at every event.

RESULT & LIMIT

What was established — and what was not

The research summary reports lower temporal secondary adoption than static adoption across three static-calibrated points on the 618-user common cohort. Equalizing the temporal first-generation R-eff to one was unattainable, so the fully equalized chronology effect remains inconclusive.

05 / Forecasting

Compared graph learning against meaningful baselines.

The weekly forecasting suite included majority and persistence baselines, logistic regression, MLP/GRU approaches, GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT and temporal/history-aware variants.

History-only logistic regression

0.5535

Balanced accuracy (approximately), over the leakage-controlled forecasting evaluation. The test covered 12,126 eligible user-weeks, 6,889 users and 15 weeks.

CRITICAL COMPARISON

Graph complexity did not automatically help

Graph-only forecasting was generally near 0.50 balanced accuracy. Recent user history delivered modest predictive signal, without consistent incremental gains from graph message passing once history and structural features were included.

The study also identified and corrected a monthly GRU time/batch-axis bug, invalidating the older result rather than retaining a misleading advantage.

06 / Reproducibility

Correcting results was part of the research.

The project documented data and model limitations, preserved superseded outputs and avoided silently replacing earlier work. The supplied summary reports protected-file hash checks, fixed RNG settings, source data for figures and manuscript source.

Timestamp correction

An earlier microseconds-versus-nanoseconds assumption compressed reconstructed time by approximately 1,000×. The implementation was repaired and affected temporal analyses were regenerated.

Reviewer-grade controls

Used paired simulations, randomized-time and degree-preserving nulls, bootstrapped intervals, Holm correction, sensitivity analyses and cautious language around statistically unsupported claims.

These are findings from the author's documented project experiments and research summary, not independent claims of real-world causal influence, verified political orientation or peer-reviewed publication.

Research takeaway

Static reach ≠ temporal opportunity ≠ actual transmission ≠ forecasting ability.

The project's methodological contribution is separating those mechanisms with explicit controls rather than treating them as interchangeable evidence of influence.
